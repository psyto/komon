# The Komon Manifesto

## I.

Prediction markets are the most powerful mechanism humanity has invented for aggregating information.

They outperform polls. They outperform experts. They outperform committees.

When people have skin in the game, they think harder, research deeper, and update their beliefs when reality proves them wrong. The collective intelligence that emerges is remarkable.

And we've wasted it on entertainment.

Elections. Sports. Celebrity gossip. Questions that are interesting but don't change anyone's daily life.

## II.

Your city has problems. You see them every day.

The pothole that's been there for months. The intersection where someone will eventually get hurt. The park that used to be safe.

You've reported them. Maybe you've complained at a town hall. Nothing changed.

The system isn't designed to solve problems. It's designed to process complaints.

## III.

Here's the transformation:

**Traditional prediction market:** "Will the city fix potholes this year?"

**Komon:** "Which approach will fix THIS pothole?"

The first is passive observation. The second is active prescription.

The first generates information. The second generates **actionable intelligence**—and rewards the people who provide it.

This is prediction markets graduating from entertainment to infrastructure.

## IV.

What changes when we point this mechanism at civic problems?

**The feedback loop inverts.** Instead of citizens complaining and officials deciding, citizens propose AND evaluate. The signal isn't "we're unhappy"—it's "here's what we believe will work, backed by money."

**A new role emerges.** Not activists. Not bureaucrats. *Direction traders*—people who develop expertise in what actually gets problems solved in their neighborhood.

**Local knowledge becomes valuable.** The retired engineer's insight. The shop owner who's watched three fixes fail. The city planner who knows which department has budget. That knowledge becomes monetizable.

**Incentives align toward outcomes.** Everyone in the system—problem posters, direction proposers, stakers, verifiers—only wins when problems actually get solved.

## V.

Here's what we believe:

**You know things.** The person who walks past a problem every day understands it better than the consultant who flies in for a study.

**Ideas should compete.** Not in comment threads where the loudest voice wins, but in markets where bad ideas cost money and good ideas pay off.

**Judgment has value.** Knowing *what will work*—not what sounds good, not what's politically safe—is a skill. It should be rewarded.

**Outcomes matter.** Not meetings held. Not reports filed. Did the problem get solved?

## VI.

Komon is simple:

1. **See a problem.** Post it.
2. **Have an idea?** Propose a direction.
3. **Think something will work?** Stake on it.
4. **Be right.** Get paid.

Your track record follows you. Good judgment compounds. Bad judgment is visible.

No credentials required. No connections needed. Just be right more than you're wrong.

## VII.

This isn't about replacing government. It's about creating signal.

When 500 people stake $10,000 on "install a speed bump" and the market shows 75% confidence, that's information. It's evidence that people with skin in the game believe this will work.

That signal can inform budgets, prioritize maintenance, or give officials cover to try something new.

We're not asking government to step aside. We're asking it to listen to a better signal.

## VIII.

The future we want:

- **Local knowledge matters.** The retired engineer's insight has weight.
- **Good judgment is recognized.** People with track records become trusted voices.
- **Problems get solved.** Not studied, not discussed—solved.
- **Anyone can contribute.** No gatekeepers. No credentials. Just outcomes.

## IX.

Shared problems deserve shared solutions.

That's not a slogan. It's a design principle.

If a problem affects everyone who uses that road, everyone should be able to contribute to solving it. And if someone correctly identifies the solution, they should be rewarded—whether they're a city planner or a high school student.

## X.

We're taking the most powerful information-aggregation mechanism ever invented and pointing it at something that matters.

Not elections that happen once every few years. Not sports scores that reset every season.

The problems you walk past every day. The ones that make you think "someone should fix this."

That someone might be you. Not by fixing it yourself—but by correctly identifying what will work.

It might not work. Markets have limits. Coordination is hard. We might be wrong about all of it.

But we're not going to find out by filing another report.

---

**Direct the future. Get rewarded.**

*This is Komon.*
